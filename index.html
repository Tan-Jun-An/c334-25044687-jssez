From planning to action

The SETUP framework

A five-part guide to planning an expansion into the JS-SEZ.

Playbook, pp. 6–7

  1. S

    Select your zone strategically

    Compare the nine zones and their targeted activities. Choose the place that best fits your business needs.

  2. E

    Evaluate incentives and requirements

    Check which incentives and facilitation support may fit your activities, sector and investment plans.

  3. T

    Take the right legal steps

    Choose a business structure, register the entity, obtain required licences, and plan tax, banking and customs compliance.

  4. U

    Utilise bilateral support

    Use Singapore's JS-SEZ Project Office and Malaysia's IMFC-J for set-up guidance. SMF and the Federation of Malaysian Manufacturers can support market entry and partnerships.

  5. P

    Prepare your talent and supply chain

    Plan workforce needs, training and cross-border operations, and build local supply chain partnerships.

Plan carefully

Incentives and tax watch-outs

Incentives can support expansion, but eligibility and compliance depend on the activity and operating model.

What the playbook highlights

Potential incentives and support

  • Corporate tax: 5% for 10 or 15 years for qualifying activities. The overview cites capital investment, excluding land, of at least RM500 million and RM1 billion in manufacturing.
  • Knowledge workers: 5% personal income tax is listed for knowledge workers.
  • Other support: sector-specific incentives, grants and facilitation support from both sides.

Playbook, p. 3

Check before you commit

Structure, track and document

  • Incentive conditions differ between Singapore and Malaysia. Make the business model and activities fit the criteria; incentives should follow the operating model.
  • Cross-border work can create a permanent establishment and tax obligations. Define activities in each location and keep records.
  • Price related-party transactions at arm's length and keep supporting records.
  • Track employees' workdays, locations and activities. The JS-SEZ does not override domestic tax rules or remove immigration obligations.

Playbook, pp. 14–17

People on the move

Passes for staff and business visitors

The playbook describes different routes for expatriate roles, temporary professional work and business travel.

Playbook, pp. 28–29

Employment

Employment Pass (EP)

MIDA assesses expatriate-position applications for eligible sectors and approved regional establishments/offices. The playbook lists these minimum basic salaries by category:

Category I
RM10,000
Category II
RM5,000–RM9,999
Category III
RM3,000–RM4,999

The playbook states revised requirements take effect on 1 June 2026. Confirm current criteria with the relevant agency.

Temporary professional work

Professional Visit Pass (PVP)

For foreign professionals providing specific services or training on behalf of an overseas company. The playbook describes a pass of up to 12 months that is not extendable, including for project work such as machine installation and stabilisation.

Business travel

Investor Pass

Introduced in 2025 for foreign business travellers in three categories: new investors, investors in the pipeline and existing investors. The playbook describes a multi-entry facility for up to 12 months, with stays of up to six months at a time and an option to extend for a further six months, subject to requirements.

Pass details summarised from the playbook appendix. Always confirm current terms and application requirements with the relevant Malaysian agencies. Playbook, pp. 27–29
